A year ago, if you asked someone to name an Indian AI company, you'd probably get one answer: Krutrim. Today, that list has grown so fast that I had to cut it at 100, and even then I left out good teams.

2026 is the year Indian AI stopped being a side story. Fractal Analytics listed on the NSE and BSE on 16 February, becoming the first pure-play AI company from India to go public. That same week, at the India AI Impact Summit in New Delhi, Sarvam shipped two open-source models trained from scratch in India, and Mumbai's Neysa announced a Blackstone-led $1.2 billion raise for GPU cloud. By July we had three new AI unicorns: Neysa, Sarvam and Emergent.

The money tells the same story. According to Inc42, Indian AI startups raised **$676 million across 57 deals in H1 2026**, more than four times the $162 million raised in H1 2025, even as overall startup funding fell 9%. Q3 added another $438 million across 35 deals.

I work on AI platforms for a living (gateways, MCP integrations, SRE agents), so I wanted a list that's useful to builders, not just investors. Here's how I put it together.

## How I picked the 100

- **Built in India, or by Indian founders with real teams here.** A few, like Uniphore or Smallest.ai, are headquartered in the US but build heavily from India.
- **Still a startup.** Listed companies (Fractal, E2E Networks) and acquired ones are out.
- **AI is the product, not a feature.** If you removed the model, the company wouldn't exist.
- **Facts checked as of 12 November 2026.** Funding numbers come from company announcements and outlets like Inc42, TechCrunch, Entrackr and Business Today. Startup numbers move fast, so check before you quote them.

The list isn't a ranking. I've grouped it into ten buckets so you can jump to the part of the stack you care about.

## 1. Foundation models and sovereign AI

The IndiaAI Mission picked 12 teams across two rounds to build home-grown models. This is the most capital-hungry corner of the ecosystem, and the one with the most national pride riding on it.

- **1. Sarvam AI (Bengaluru)**: India's flagship sovereign-AI lab. Raised $234M in June 2026 at a $1.5B valuation, with HCLTech putting in $150M, and open-sourced its Sarvam 30B and 105B models.
- **2. Krutrim (Bengaluru)**: Bhavish Aggarwal's AI venture and India's first AI unicorn. After a rough patch, it pivoted to AI cloud services in May 2026 and reported its first annual profit.
- **3. Soket AI Labs (Gurugram)**: Building EKA, an open-source 120B-parameter Indic model under the IndiaAI Mission, aimed at sectors like defence, healthcare and education.
- **4. Gnani.ai (Bengaluru)**: Voice-first AI that launched Inya VoiceOS, a 5B-parameter voice-to-voice model, and raised a $10M Series B led by Aavishkaar Capital.
- **5. Gan.ai (Bengaluru)**: Started in personalised video; now building a 70B multilingual model focused on text-to-speech under the IndiaAI Mission.
- **6. CoRover (Bengaluru)**: The team behind BharatGPT, with conversational AI deployed for large public-sector clients.
- **7. Genloop (Bengaluru)**: Picked in the IndiaAI Mission's second round to build Indian LLMs and small language models.
- **8. Shodh AI (Bengaluru)**: Research-first lab, also in the second IndiaAI round, building domain-specific models for science and enterprise.
- **9. Zenteiq (Bengaluru)**: Scientific-AI startup selected under the IndiaAI foundation-model pillar.
- **10. Avataar.ai (Bengaluru)**: Known for generative 3D for e-commerce; now one of the 12 IndiaAI foundation-model teams.
- **11. 169Pi**: A young lab building general-purpose AI models; featured on Inc42's AI Startups To Watch in January 2026.

## What this list actually tells us

Put all 100 side by side and a few patterns jump out.

**Bengaluru still dominates, but it's not the whole story.** Zinnov's March 2026 report on India's top 100 AI startups found 59 of them based in Bengaluru. My list looks similar. But Rocket in Surat, Netrasemi in Thiruvananthapuram and Mihup in Kolkata show that good AI teams no longer need a Koramangala address.

**Capital is concentrating.** A handful of companies (Neysa, Sarvam, Emergent, Uniphore) took a large share of 2026's money. Most others on this list are at seed to Series B, raising $5–30 million at a time. That gap matters: training models and buying GPUs is expensive, and India still has few late-stage AI investors.

**Voice and agents are where Indian startups have an edge.** India's languages and the habit of calling rather than typing create problems global players don't solve well. That's why you see Gnani, Bolna, Smallest.ai, Sarvam and a dozen others building for real phone calls in Hindi, Tamil and Hinglish.

**The infra layer is finally real.** Two years ago, an Indian team that wanted GPUs had to go to AWS or Azure. Now there's Neysa, Yotta and Krutrim's cloud, plus Simplismart, TrueFoundry and Portkey to run models on top. As someone who builds AI platforms, this is the shift I find most exciting.

**Not every bet will work.** Krutrim's year (layoffs, a consumer app pulled offline, then a pivot to cloud) is a reminder that big announcements aren't the same as product-market fit. Some names on this list won't be here next year. That's normal.

If you're a developer, my advice is simple: try the open models (Sarvam's are on Hugging Face), play with the infra tools, and follow the voice-AI teams. That's where India is building something the rest of the world will copy.
